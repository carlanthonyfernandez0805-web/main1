INTERPOL Notices

CCF Access Request

INTERPOL will not tell you if a notice exists — you have to ask.

Direct answer: A CCF access request is a formal application to the Commission for the Control of INTERPOL’s Files, asking whether any data concerning you is held in INTERPOL’s systems. INTERPOL does not proactively notify individuals of a notice, so many people first learn of one through a border stop or bank query — an access request is the direct route to confirming what, if anything, actually exists.
01

No Direct Notification

INTERPOL does not notify individuals directly when a notice or diffusion is issued against them.

02

Formal CCF Submission

The request is submitted directly to the Commission for the Control of INTERPOL’s Files.

03

Confirms What Exists

The response confirms whether data is held, and can provide the basis for a further removal request.

04

Often the First Step

Frequently the necessary first step before pursuing a full removal or deletion application.

Frequently Asked

Common Questions

How long does a CCF access request take?

Timelines vary, but this initial confirmation step is generally faster than a full removal application.

What happens if the CCF confirms a notice exists?

You can then pursue a full removal or deletion application on the applicable grounds.

Can I request access without a lawyer?

Yes, but professional guidance can help ensure the request is framed to support any follow-on removal application.
