Direct answer: an international arrest usually happens one of two ways — a formal provisional arrest request from the country pursuing you, or a local arrest triggered by an INTERPOL Red Notice or diffusion already on file. Provisional arrest is meant to hold a person only until a full extradition request arrives, within a deadline set by treaty — typically 40 to 90 days. What happens in the days immediately after arrest often shapes the entire case.

How It Happens

How International Arrests Happen

An arrest abroad is rarely the first step in a case — it is usually the visible result of a request or alert filed earlier.

01

Provisional Arrest Request

A formal, urgent request from the requesting state asking local authorities to arrest someone pending a full extradition request.

02

INTERPOL Notices & Diffusions

A Red Notice or diffusion already on file can prompt an arrest at a border or during a routine police check, treaty or not.

03

Formal Request Deadline

Most treaties require the full extradition request to arrive within a set window — commonly 40, 60, or 90 days — or the person must be released.

04

Dual Criminality & Urgency

Provisional arrest generally requires the conduct to be a crime locally too, and evidence that the case is genuinely urgent.

After the Arrest

What Happens After an International Arrest

The first hearing sets the tone for everything that follows. What is decided there is rarely reversed easily later.

  • 01
    Initial HearingA local court confirms identity, explains the basis for the arrest, and sets a timetable — this is not where guilt or the merits are decided.
  • 02
    Bail & DetentionExtradition cases often carry a presumption against bail on flight-risk grounds; a well-prepared bail application can still succeed with the right conditions.
  • 03
    Consular NotificationYou are generally entitled to have your embassy or consulate notified and to receive a visit, regardless of the charges.
  • 04
    Right to CounselYou are entitled to legal representation from the outset — before signing anything or agreeing to any simplified surrender procedure.

Frequently Asked

Common Questions

Can I get bail after an international arrest?

It depends heavily on the jurisdiction and the alleged offence, but bail is often possible with the right conditions and legal argument, even where a flight-risk presumption applies.

What is the difference between provisional arrest and a formal extradition request?

Provisional arrest is a stopgap measure to hold someone urgently; the formal request is the complete legal package that must follow within a set deadline for the case to continue.

Can I be arrested based on a Red Notice alone?

In many countries, yes — a Red Notice can prompt a local arrest even before a formal extradition request has been submitted.

What should I do immediately after being arrested abroad?

Ask for a lawyer and consular notification before answering questions or signing any documents, and avoid agreeing to a simplified or voluntary surrender until you have taken advice.
